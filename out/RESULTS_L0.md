## 4. H2 — does the *parent's* code-mixing predict the reply? (the study's hypothesis)

| Parent CMI tercile | n | mean parent CMI | % reply body-shaming | % reply aggression | % X |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 209 | 0.0 | 9.1% | 17.2% | 12.9% |

| high | 209 | 26.4 | 4.8% | 7.2% | 7.2% |

Odds ratio per +10 parent CMI, controlling for parent length and the reply's own CMI. X excluded.

| Outcome / model | OR | 95% CI | p | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reply body-shaming — pooled | 0.96 | 0.60–1.54 | 0.855 | 565 |
| Reply body-shaming — within video | 1.24 | 0.67–2.31 | 0.494 | 260 (13 videos) |
| Reply aggression — pooled | 0.81 | 0.53–1.25 | 0.348 | 565 |
| Reply aggression — within video | 1.47 | 0.95–2.27 | 0.084 | 340 (17 videos) |
| Reply aggression, parent mixed (yes/no) — pooled | 0.37 | 0.14–0.97 | 0.043 | 565 |
| Reply aggression, parent mixed (yes/no) — within video | 2.22 | 0.62–8.00 | 0.221 | 340 (17 videos) |

**Result: null, and the raw trend is a confound.** Pooled, replies to code-mixed parents look less aggressive (OR 0.37, p = 0.043). Within the same video the sign flips and the effect is no longer significant (OR 2.22, p = 0.221). Comment sections that are more English are also calmer; code-mixing is not what makes them calmer. The within-video estimates all point the other way (aggression per +10 parent CMI: p = 0.08) — not significant, but worth re-testing with more data.

## 5. H3 — swearing (IV-3)

After the profanity-matcher fix (see `src/profanity.py`), only **14 of 786 comments (1.8%)** contain a swear word.

| Label | with swear | without |
|---|---|---|
| 0 Neither | 2 | 602 |
| 1 Profane, not aggressive | 4 | 15 |
| 2 Other aggression | 5 | 43 |
| 3 Body-shaming | 3 | 46 |
| X Cannot judge | 0 | 66 |

Swear present vs aggression (2/3): Fisher exact OR = 9.24, p < 0.001.
Of the 14 judged comments containing a swear, 8 were aggressive (2/3) and 6 were not (4 profane-not-aggressive, 2 neither).

**Result: too sparse to test.** Swearing is rare in these comment sections, so IV-3 has almost no variance. What exists supports rule 7 (profanity ≠ aggression): swearing raises the odds of aggression, but close to half of swearing comments were not aggressive.

## 6. Secondary findings

**Accommodation.** Reply CMI tracks parent CMI: Spearman ρ = 0.12, p = 0.003. Weak but reliable — repliers partly match the language mix they reply to.

**What makes a comment unjudgeable (X)?** Being a reply: OR 4.1 (1.7–10.2), p = 0.002. Code-mixing: OR 0.91, p = 0.234. Difficulty comes from missing context, not from mixing languages — the motivation for L1.

**Appearance vocabulary misses body-shaming.** 15 of 49 body-shaming comments (31%) contain no appearance word; only 17% of appearance-word comments are body-shaming. Keyword filtering would lose a third of the phenomenon.

## 7. Limits

- **Power.** 49 body-shaming comments (42 among judged replies). The H2 interval cannot exclude a moderate effect: report H2 as *inconclusive*, not *no effect*.
- **Single annotator**, no reliability figure yet (test–retest and inter-annotator α pending).
- **Sample.** Videos found by keyword search and capped at 30 comments each; not representative of YouTube Hinglish.
- **Blinding.** IVs were seen before L1 (see disclosure).
